# mpDAO vote contracts — source audit

Date: 2026-11-25

Reviewer: Grok 4.7, a language model trained by SpaceXAI, running as the coding agent in this session.

Scope: `meta-vote-contract` and `mpip-contract` in this repository, read from source. This is not a review of mainnet state. It does not check who the live owner and operator are, how the price bot chooses a price, or whether `mpdao_avail_to_sell` is backed by tokens held apart from user locks.

Each finding has an id. Disposition is open unless noted. A finding is closed by a code change or by a written note on this item saying why the current behavior is accepted.

## Findings

### #1 — Buys use the raw price and draw on one shared mpDAO balance

Severity: high. Actor: any account, if the operator posts a price taken from a pool that account can move. The loss reaches other users' locks only when the sale inventory was counted out of the same balance.

Where: `meta-vote-contract/src/buy_and_lock.rs` (`update_mpdao_prices`, `receive_sell_lock_and_vote`, `update_mpdao_avail_to_sell`). `MpdaoPrice::is_fresh` accepts a price for 20 minutes.

The contract never reads a pool. Only the operator can call `update_mpdao_prices`, and the buy path stores that number as-is. The comment on that function says the operator should price mpDAO from the mpDAO/stNEAR pool. If the operator's bot copies that pool's spot price, any account that can trade the pool can choose the number the bot publishes.

`mpdao_per_token_e9` is how much mpDAO one whole payment token buys, scaled by 1e9. A higher number means mpDAO is cheaper. The buy uses that raw figure for 20 minutes. There is no median, no daily move cap, and no size cap on the buy. The same update is recorded for sells, and sells do have those guards. A crash (mpDAO cheaper, so mpDAO-per-token goes up) is accepted immediately on the sell side too, which makes the contract pay less USDT. Selling the position straight back is a loss. The open direction is the buy.

The undisturbed pool says 1 USDT buys 1 mpDAO. A trade that moves a thin pool until 1 USDT buys 10 mpDAO, followed by the bot posting that print, lets a later buy of 100 USDT lock 1,000 mpDAO. The contract decrements `mpdao_avail_to_sell` by 1,000 and creates a normal locking position. After the unbond, `withdraw` sends that mpDAO out of the contract's token balance. The 100 USDT sits in the buy reserve, which the operator can forward to the owner.

The operator does not have to choose a false price. A bot that samples spot and posts it is enough. This review did not look at that bot. If it posts a median, pauses on a jump, or refuses a price that moved too far from the last one, the pool trade never becomes the buy price. The contract itself will store whatever positive number the operator sends.

The only on-chain debit for the mpDAO is `mpdao_avail_to_sell`. The owner sets that number. It is not checked against the token balance, or against locks, claimables, and free balance. The loss is the sale inventory. It reaches other users' locks when that inventory was counted out of the same balance that backs those locks.

#### Recommended fix

Cap the buy in the direction that gives the buyer more mpDAO. Accept a lower `mpdao_per_token_e9` immediately, so a buyer can only receive fewer tokens when the print moves. Cap a rise at 10% from the previous accepted day, keep the 7-day window, and charge the buyer the smaller of today's capped value and the median. That is the opposite of the sell guard, which takes the larger number so the contract pays out less USDT. Add a daily cap on mpDAO sold, matching the $2,000 USDT cap on sells. Increase `mpdao_avail_to_sell` only when mpDAO is actually transferred in for sale, and refuse a buy unless the token balance still covers existing locks, free balances, claimables, and the inventory that would remain.

#### Mitigation

The buyback bot clamps the number it posts from `updateBuyAndLockMpdaoPrices`, and keeps that history in `buy-price-guard.json`. A richer mpDAO price is posted immediately. A cheaper print can rise at most 10% from the previous accepted day, a same-day spike is capped against the day's open, and the posted price is the smaller of today and the 7-day median. The first saved print for a symbol is the spot. The existing pause still posts the raw spot with buys paused when the Rhea price is under 70% of the 5-day average (50% on testnet), and that crash is not written into the buy history. A crash smaller than that floor moves the sell price at most 10% toward cheaper mpDAO that day. The contract's sell guard, lock discount, and $2,000 daily USDT cap bound that.

`update_mpdao_avail_to_sell` now accepts the owner or the operator and requires one yocto. On the same pass as the safe top-up, the bot sets that counter from `operator.mpdao-vote.near`. The cap is the mpDAO in the vote contract plus the safe, minus NEAR locks, claimable mpDAO, unlocked claimable mpDAO, and a 1,000,000 mpDAO floor. Locks still on `meta-vote.near` are not reserved. That contract is deprecated and can no longer mint locks. The voter list is read fresh, because a stale snapshot would leave new locks out of the reserve and set the cap too high. A dry run logs the number and does not send it. The daily cap on mpDAO bought, and crediting the counter only from a real deposit, are not part of this change.

### #2 — Realizing an Ethereum mirror creates a withdrawable lock with no deposit

Severity: high. Actor: operator.

Where: `operator_mirror_lps` in `meta-vote-contract/src/lib.rs`, `realize_mirror` in `meta-vote-contract/src/realize_mirror.rs`.

`operator_mirror_lps` writes locking positions for `{eth}.evmp.near` without receiving mpDAO. `realize_mirror` copies those amounts onto the confirmed NEAR delegate as ordinary locking positions. The delegate can unlock them and withdraw mpDAO from the contract balance.

#### Mitigation

Accepted. Both calls are operator-only. Mirroring will be deprecated: there is no more mpDAO on Ethereum, so there is nothing left to mirror.

### #3 — The previous governance contract can mint locking positions

Severity: high. Actor: the account in `prev_governance_contract`. The owner sets that account with `set_prev_gov_contract`.

Where: `migration_create_lps` in `meta-vote-contract/src/lib.rs`.

The predecessor can pass any `voter_id` and a list of `(amount, days)`. Each entry becomes a locking position. The amount is the old 24-decimal figure divided by `1e18`. Nothing in this contract records that a position was migrated once. The associated-user-data write is stored under the predecessor account, not under `voter_id`.

#### Mitigation

Accepted. On `mpdao-vote.near`, read 2026-11-25, `prev_governance_contract` is `""`. `migration_create_lps` requires the predecessor account to equal that string, and no account id is empty, so the call reverts. The owner can put an account back with `set_prev_gov_contract`.

### #4 — EVM delegation is an operator allowlist

Severity: high. Actor: operator.

Where: `operator_confirm_delegated_evm_address`, `delegated_claim_stnear`, `delegated_claim_unlocked_mpdao`, `vote_delegated` in `meta-vote-contract/src/evm_delegate.rs`.

The contract stores the signature and does not check it. The confirmed NEAR account can claim that Ethereum address's stNEAR and unlocked mpDAO, and can vote as the pseudo account `{address}.evmp.near`. The check that the pre-delegation still matches `(expected account, expected signature)` holds only if the operator verified the signature off-chain.

#### Mitigation

Accepted. Confirming a delegation is operator-only. The delegation functions will be deprecated soon.

### #5 — Closing an MPIP marks it executed without a majority

Severity: high. Actor: any voter, once the operator runs the normal close.

Where: `close_proposal_if_quorum` and `internal_is_quorum_reached` in `mpip-contract`. `get_proposal_state` returns `Executed` as soon as `proposal.executed` is set.

Quorum is `for + abstain`. The close sets `executed` when that quorum holds. It does not require `for > against`. `get_proposal_vote_succeeded` has the majority check, and the state function never reaches it after `executed` is set. Abstain votes can push a proposal that lost on the count into `Executed`.

#### Recommended fix

Set `executed` in `close_proposal_if_quorum` only when `internal_is_quorum_reached` and `get_proposal_vote_succeeded` are both true. A proposal that reaches quorum with `for` less than or equal to `against` stays unexecuted. After the voting period, `get_proposal_state` then returns `Rejected`. Abstain votes still count toward quorum.

#### Status

Fixed in 742e227 on 2026-11-25. No state migration.

### #6 — The operator can set the MPIP quorum denominator

Severity: high. Actor: operator.

Where: `operator_set_total_voting_power` in `meta-vote-contract/src/lib.rs`. MPIP reads `get_total_voting_power` when a voting period starts.

The function exists to repair a hand-maintained counter. The value it writes is the number MPIP snapshots for quorum. A value near zero makes quorum trivial.

#### Recommended fix

Stop accepting a number from the operator. Replace the body of `operator_set_total_voting_power` with a paged recount: each call adds `sum_locked_vp` for the next page of voters, the same sum as `get_locked_voting_power_page`, into a pending total. The call that reaches the last voter stores that total in `total_voting_power` and clears the pending total. The operator can start and continue the recount, and cannot choose the figure MPIP snapshots. This adds a pending sum and the next voter index.

If the one-time repair is already applied and the counter no longer needs a manual write, delete the function instead. That is a code change with no new state. The counter then moves only from lock, unlock, and vote updates.

#### Status

Deleted in 742e227 on 2026-11-25. No state change. Nothing in the bot or the UI called it, and `mpdao-vote.near` has no receipts of it.

### #7 — A deposit for another account can fill their lock slots

Severity: medium. Actor: any account. The attacker spends their own mpDAO.

Where: `ft_on_transfer` in `meta-vote-contract/src/deposit.rs`, the `["voter_id", days]` message. The same beneficiary field exists on the buy-and-lock path.

Each distinct duration takes one of `max_locking_positions` slots. Filling the slots blocks new durations for that account until each gifted position is unlocked and cleared.

#### Recommended fix

When the account that pays is not the account that receives the lock, require at least 1,000 mpDAO. That covers the `["voter_id", days]` message and a buy-and-lock `beneficiary` other than the sender. A deposit for the sender keeps `min_deposit_amount`. The check is a constant, so there is no new state. On mainnet, filling all 32 lock slots for someone else would cost 32,000 mpDAO.

#### Status

Fixed in 742e227 on 2026-11-25. No state change. A lock paid by someone other than the recipient must be at least 1,000 mpDAO. A deposit for the sender keeps `min_deposit_amount`.

### #8 — The vote freeze matches a substring

Severity: medium. Actor: any voter, during a freeze the operator armed. The operator also chooses the filter.

Where: `assert_votes_not_frozen` and `set_lock_in_vote_filters` in `meta-vote-contract/src/lib.rs`.

A vote is frozen only when its id contains `" #{n} "` for the configured number. An id that does not use that shape can still be reduced. `set_lock_in_vote_filters` has no one-yocto check.

#### Status

Discarded. Only the operator can arm the freeze. The tally is the exact vote id. A vote whose id does not contain `" #{n} "` was never part of the total for an id that does, so reducing it does not change that proposal's count.

### #9 — The operator can restart the undelegation clock

Severity: medium. Actor: operator.

Where: `refresh_vote_timestamps` in `meta-vote-contract/src/lib.rs`.

Refreshing a voter's `delegated` or `metastaking.app` timestamps restarts the 7-day wait for reducing or removing those votes. The operator can call it for any voter. `operator_force_undelegate` skips the wait in the other direction.

#### Status

Discarded. Vote expiration and vote delegation are deprecated.

### #10 — A test address writes an already-stale vote timestamp

Severity: low. Actor: any account, limited to votes whose contract address is exactly `**stale***`.

Where: `store_vote_timestamp` in `meta-vote-contract/src/timestamp_utils.rs`.

That address stores `DEFAULT_TIMESTAMP - 60 days - 1s` instead of the block time. `verify_vote_is_stale` is then true, so `remove_stale_votes_by_list` will drop the vote. It does not backdate a vote on any other contract address.

#### Status

Discarded. No contract address is `**stale***`. The hook runs only when a vote uses that exact string.

### #11 — Token setup has no one-yocto guard

Severity: low. Actor: a function-call key on the owner. A full-access key is not required.

Where: `set_token_info` and `enable_token` in `meta-vote-contract/src/buy_and_lock.rs`.

Those methods change which tokens can buy mpDAO. They check the owner. `update_mpdao_avail_to_sell` now requires one yocto and accepts the owner or the operator.

#### Status

Fixed in 742e227 on 2026-11-25. No state change. `set_token_info` and `enable_token` require one yocto. The bot already attaches 1 yocto on those calls.

### #12 — Anyone can sweep surplus NEAR and the NEAR accounting follows the sweep

Severity: low. Actor: any account. The NEAR is sent to the owner, not to the caller.

Where: `transfer_extra_near_balance` in `meta-vote-contract/src/buy_and_lock.rs`.

The call sends the balance above storage cost plus 5 NEAR to the owner. It also subtracts that amount from the NEAR token's `amount_received`, including NEAR that was not a purchase (refresh fees, registration).

#### Status

As designed. The NEAR is sent to the owner, not to the caller.

### #13 — Removing a self-delegation reloaded a stale voter and could stick another unvote

Severity: low. Actor: the voter who still has a legacy self-delegation (`delegated` vote whose target is themselves). New delegations are rejected.

#### Status

Fixed in 742e227 on 2026-11-25.

Where: `is_self_delegation` in `meta-vote-contract/src/lib.rs`, used by `unvote` (via `internal_remove_voting_position`) and by the decrease branch of `rebalance`.

`internal_remove_delegated_voting_power` loads the delegate from storage and saves that copy. For a self-delegation the delegate is the voter the caller is already editing. The reload could shrink another vote in the global tally, and the caller's later save put that vote back, so a later unvote of it panicked. Self-delegation is a wash: the same amount is received and spent. Removal now drops the vote and the global tally only. It does not credit available voting power and does not reload the voter. A delegation to a different account still reloads that delegate and claws back votes they had already cast.

Tests: `unvoting_a_self_delegation_keeps_other_votes` and `reducing_a_self_delegation_does_not_free_voting_power` in `meta-vote-contract/src/tests_rebalance.rs`.
